The right starting point depends on what you are trying to build, improve, assess or budget.

Some clients need help organizing a floor plan. Others are considering renovation, exploring an exterior appearance, reviewing structural behaviour or preparing an initial cost picture. We discuss the requirement first, then define the appropriate scope and deliverables.

Share your project type, location, approximate size, reference drawings or photographs and the outcome you want. This helps make the first conversation more useful.

01

Building Layout Planning

Plan spaces around the way people will use them. The work may include understanding the brief, arranging rooms, considering circulation, improving functional space utilization and developing floor-plan concepts for discussion.

Typical discussion points: plot dimensions and orientation, number of floors, room requirements, family or business needs, access, privacy, ventilation and future flexibility.

Potential deliverables: requirement summary, layout options, room arrangement concepts and floor-plan documentation as agreed in the project scope.

Enquire about layout planning
02

Building Renovation

Think through how an existing building can be improved before committing to changes. Renovation planning starts with understanding the current space, the problems to solve and the desired outcome.

Typical discussion points: existing layout, space constraints, modernization goals, interior updates, exterior improvements, available drawings and known building issues.

Potential deliverables: renovation concepts, space-improvement ideas, design direction and documentation appropriate to the agreed scope. Site inspection and specialist assessments must be arranged where required.

Enquire about renovation
03

Interior and Exterior Design

Develop a coherent visual direction while keeping usability in view. Interior concepts can explore living rooms, bedrooms, kitchens, bathrooms, lighting, furniture placement and practical storage.

Exterior concepts can explore building elevation, facade composition, proportions, finishing ideas, material palettes and the overall architectural appearance.

Helpful inputs: room or building dimensions, photographs, preferred styles, reference images, functional needs and an indicative budget range.

Enquire about design
04

STAAD.Pro Structural Analysis

Structural analysis workflows can involve model preparation, defining structural elements and loads, evaluating structural behaviour, reviewing analysis outputs and preparing technical documentation.

The suitability and accuracy of any analysis depend on complete project inputs, modelling assumptions, applicable codes and qualified engineering review. STAAD.Pro is a tool used for relevant structural analysis workflows; software output alone is not construction approval.

All structural models, load calculations, designs and recommendations must be independently checked and approved by appropriately qualified professionals before construction.

Enquire about structural analysis
05

Construction Estimation

Build an initial understanding of quantities and potential project costs. Estimation may include material quantity calculations, preliminary budgeting, cost documentation and BOQ preparation where included in the agreed service.

Typical inputs: drawings, dimensions, specifications, material preferences, project location and the level of detail required.

Estimates are based on stated assumptions and available rates. Actual costs can vary with market prices, site conditions, design changes, labour, transport and contractor scope.

Enquire about estimation
